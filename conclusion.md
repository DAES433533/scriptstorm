Across all three meteorological events the July 2023 Western U.S. heatwave, the 2011 tornado outbreak, and the November 2022 Buffalo lake effect snowstorm our analysis using ERA5 reanalysis data, satellite observations, and radar imagery highlights how different atmospheric setups lead to dramatically different impacts.

For the July 2023 heatwave, ERA5 cloud cover fields showed widespread clear skies across the western United States a signal that was confirmed using GOES 18 true color imagery. The combination of clear skies strong solar heating and elevated 850 mb temperatures aligned with the presence of a pronounced 500 hPa ridge, which explains the intense and persistent heat experienced across the region. Wind vectors at 850 mb also supported the stagnant subsident air mass typical of strong ridging and heatwaves.

During the April 2011 tornado outbreak, the ERA5 fields similarly captured the classic severe weather environment. Surface temperatures were high across the Southeast and central U.S., and CAPE values ranged from 500 to 2000 J/kg, indicating sufficient instability for tornadic supercells. The 850 mb level showed strong southerly flow transporting warm, moist Gulf air northward fueling storm development. Sea level pressure fields revealed a deep low pressure system, with central pressures near 980 hPa. Radar imagery over Alabama, especially near Tuscaloosa confirmed the severity of the event, showing well defined hook echoes and strong rotational signatures associated with the EF4 tornado that caused catastrophic damage.

The November 2022 Buffalo lake effect snowstorm displayed an entirely different but equally impressive set of atmospheric dynamics. ERA5 snow depth analyses indicated over 30 inches of accumulation just south of Buffalo. Radar data showed the persistent and nearly stationary lake effect band that lasted nearly two days. Geopotential heights dropped from around the 540 dam line to near 530 dam between November 18 to 19 signaling a colder air mass settling into the region. The 850 mb temperatures and westerly flow supported ideal lake effect conditions cold air aloft passing over the warm lake surface generating intense convection and extremely localized snowfall.

The three events highlight the wide range of atmospheric conditions that produce significant weather impacts across the United States. From extreme heat to violent tornadoes to localized lake effect snow, ERA5, satellite imagery, and radar data collectively reveal the underlying dynamics that shaped each outcome.
